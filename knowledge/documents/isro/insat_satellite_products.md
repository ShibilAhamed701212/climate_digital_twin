# INSAT-3DR Satellite Products and Applications

## Overview

INSAT-3DR is a dedicated meteorological satellite operated by ISRO, positioned at 74°E longitude. It provides continuous observations of the Indian region for weather forecasting and climate monitoring. The satellite carries a six-channel imager and a 19-channel sounder for atmospheric profiling.

## Key Products

### Rainfall Estimation
The INSAT-3DR provides quantitative precipitation estimates (QPE) using infrared (IR) and water vapor (WV) channels. The INSAT Multispectral Rainfall Algorithm (IMSRA) combines IR brightness temperature with microwave-derived rain rates. Estimates are calibrated against IMD rain gauge data at daily and monthly scales. The spatial resolution is 4 km for IR channels and 8 km for WV channels.

### Sea Surface Temperature
SST products are derived from thermal infrared channels at 4 km spatial resolution using split-window algorithms. Accuracy is within 0.5°C. These products are used for monsoon prediction, cyclone tracking, and ocean-atmosphere interaction studies. Daily and weekly composite products are available.

### Cloud Properties
Cloud top temperature (CTT) and cloud top pressure (CTP) are derived from IR window channels. These are used to estimate convective intensity, rainfall potential, and severe weather indicators. The INSAT-3DR can detect overshooting cloud tops, which are precursors to severe thunderstorms.

### Insolation Products
Surface insolation (downward shortwave radiation) is estimated using visible channel measurements. These products support agricultural applications, solar energy estimation, and evapotranspiration modeling. Spatial resolution is 4 km with half-hourly updates.

## Applications

Real-time weather monitoring enables nowcasting of thunderstorms and heavy rainfall events. Cyclone warning and tracking uses INSAT-3DR imagery to estimate cyclone intensity, track movement, and predict landfall. Monsoon monitoring tracks the progress of the monsoon and identifies active/break periods. Agricultural advisory services use satellite-derived rainfall estimates and vegetation indices for crop monitoring. Flood warning systems integrate satellite rainfall estimates with hydrological models for flood forecasting.
