# Karnataka Climate Profile

## Geographic Context

Karnataka is located in southwestern India between 11.5°N and 18.5°N latitude, 74.0°E and 78.5°E longitude. The state spans 191,791 square kilometers and includes coastal, malnad (hilly), and maidan (plateau) regions. The Western Ghats run parallel to the coast, significantly influencing rainfall distribution.

## Rainfall Patterns

The average annual rainfall in Karnataka is approximately 1,200 mm. Rainfall varies dramatically across regions: coastal districts like Udupi and Dakshina Kannada receive over 3,000 mm annually, while interior districts like Bijapur, Raichur, and Bellary receive less than 600 mm. The Southwest Monsoon (June-September) contributes about 80% of annual rainfall. The Northeast Monsoon (October-December) contributes 10-15% primarily in the southern districts.

## Temperature Trends

Summer temperatures range from 26°C to 38°C. April and May are the hottest months. The eastern plains (Kolar, Tumkur, Chitradurga) record the highest maximum temperatures, frequently exceeding 38°C. Winter temperatures range from 15°C to 28°C, with December and January being the coolest months. Coastal regions have moderated temperatures year-round due to maritime influence.

## Monsoon Behavior

The Southwest Monsoon typically arrives in Karnataka by June 1st and withdraws by October 15th. The monsoon onset varies: coastal regions receive the monsoon first, followed by the interior. Monsoon breaks (dry spells within the rainy season) are common and can last 7-15 days, significantly impacting rain-fed agriculture.

## Climate Zones

Karnataka has three distinct climate zones: Coastal zone (high rainfall, moderate temperatures), Malnad zone (very high rainfall, cooler temperatures due to elevation), and Maidan zone (low rainfall, extreme temperatures). Each zone requires different agricultural and water management strategies.

## Key Climate Risks

Drought: Interior districts face frequent drought conditions due to rainfall variability and dependence on monsoon. Approximately 40% of the state's districts are drought-prone. Flooding: Coastal and riverine districts (Udupi, Dakshina Kannada, North Kanara) experience flooding during heavy monsoon years. Heatwaves: Eastern plains experience heatwaves during pre-monsoon months (March-May) with temperatures exceeding 40°C. Cyclones: The Arabian Sea coast is vulnerable to cyclones, though less frequently than the Bay of Bengal coast.
